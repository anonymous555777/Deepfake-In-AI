01 / UNDERSTAND

What are deepfakes?

Deepfakes are synthetic or altered media created or modified using artificial intelligence. They can make a person appear to say or do something that never happened.

▧

AI-generated images

Images can be generated or edited to depict realistic-looking people, places or events that do not exist.

▶

Manipulated videos

Video and face-swap techniques can alter a person's appearance or create misleading scenes.

♫

Synthetic voices

AI voice systems can imitate speech patterns or generate audio that sounds like a real speaker.

02 / THE TECHNOLOGY

How does it work?

Deepfake systems learn patterns from data and use those patterns to generate or modify media. The technology can have creative uses, but it also creates risks when used deceptively.

1
Learning patterns

AI models learn visual, audio or movement patterns from example data.

2
Generating or editing

A model creates new synthetic content or changes parts of existing media.

3
Review and refinement

The output may be adjusted to look or sound more consistent and realistic.

4
Sharing and verification

Once shared online, context and reliable verification become important.

Technology is not automatically good or bad.

Its impact depends on the purpose, consent, transparency and safeguards around its use. The same broad AI field can support filmmaking, accessibility and education—or be misused to deceive people.

Important: A visual clue or an automated detector cannot prove that a piece of media is fake. Verification should use multiple sources and context.
03 / RISKS & IMPACT

Why should we care?

Misleading synthetic media can affect individuals, communities and public trust.

◉

Misinformation

Fabricated media can be used to distort events, confuse audiences or spread false claims.

♙

Identity misuse

Someone's likeness or voice may be used without permission, affecting their privacy and reputation.

⌁

Fraud and impersonation

Impersonation can be used in deceptive messages or attempts to gain trust.

◎

Loss of trust

When media can be manipulated, people may become unsure what evidence to believe.

♡

Personal harm

Non-consensual synthetic content can cause serious emotional, social and reputational harm.

⚖

Ethical challenges

Questions arise around consent, accountability, fairness and responsibility.

04 / THINK CRITICALLY

How can you verify media?

Do not rely on one visual detail. Use a careful, evidence-based process before believing or sharing surprising content.

01 Check the original source and who published it.
02 Look for independent, reputable reporting of the same event.
03 Check the date, location and surrounding context.
04 Notice unusual audio, lighting or visual inconsistencies—but treat them only as clues.
05 Use reverse-image search or trusted fact-checking resources where appropriate.
06 Treat AI detector results cautiously; tools can make mistakes.

A detector score is not a verdict. Compression, editing and low-quality recordings can affect results, and authentic media can be incorrectly flagged.

05 / PREVENTION

Pause. Verify. Protect.

Small habits can reduce the spread and impact of deceptive content.

✓

Verify before sharing

Check the source and context before forwarding emotionally charged or surprising media.

⌑

Protect personal data

Think carefully before posting sensitive photos, recordings or identifying information publicly.

⚑

Report harmful content

Use the platform's reporting tools. If someone is targeted, seek help from a trusted adult or appropriate authority.

Received a suspicious request?

Do not rely only on a familiar voice or video call. Verify through a separate, trusted contact method before taking action or sharing private information.

06 / RESPONSIBLE AI

Ethics must come first.

Responsible AI use respects people, communicates honestly and includes safeguards against foreseeable harm.

♡

Consent

Obtain permission before using someone's face, voice or personal data in synthetic media.

◇

Transparency

Clearly label AI-generated or significantly altered media when people could mistake it for reality.

⚖

Accountability

People and organisations should take responsibility for how AI systems and outputs are used.

▣

Privacy

Handle personal information carefully and avoid collecting or exposing data unnecessarily.

◎

Fairness

Consider who may be disproportionately harmed and make systems more inclusive.

盾

Safety

Build review, reporting and response processes to reduce misuse and address harm.
